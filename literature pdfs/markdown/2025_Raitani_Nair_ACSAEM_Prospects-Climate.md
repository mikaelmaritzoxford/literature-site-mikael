# 3.3. Geographic Location-Specific Optimal EgTand

**Target Degradation Rates.** Figure 4a compares the annual EY estimates for different cities across the world. The chosen cities have diverse climatic conditions, and hence, the energy yield estimates reflect the range of real-world environments where tandem solar cells are expected to perform. Here also, we used measured data (i.e., fixed-tilt spectral irradiance and climatic conditions) from the National Solar Radiation Database (NSRDB). 36 The calculations have been performed for the respective ambient conditions using the same methodology adopted for Mumbai (as detailed in the previous section). For every city, we find that the maximum annual EY is achieved for a bandgap of *E*gT∼1.71 eVwhich is different from the predictions of SQ analysis (of 1.73 eV) and efficiency calculations shown in Figure 2a (∼1.68eV). This difference is clearly due to the variation in the spectrum and the ambient conditions. The monthly EY provided in Figure 3c offers additional insights in this regard. For months with high incident irradiance, Figure 3c indicates that the optimal *E*gTis ∼1.68eV. For months with the lowest irradiance, the optimal *E* is ∼1.77eV. Consequently, the annual EY shows a peak near *E* ∼1.71 eV.

Figure 4b compares the normalized EY at various cities.

Interestingly, despite the enormous variations in climatic conditions and spectral irradiance, the variation of normalized EY with *E*gTis nearly the same for all the chosen cities. This simplifies a design challenge for P−Si 2T tandem solar cells the relative performance of such TSCs is nearly independent of geographic locations. As such, degradation rates could be the key parameter that determines economic viability.
