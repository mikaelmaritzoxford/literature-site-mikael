Recently, the industrial viability of RPD-SnO*x* has been highlighted for its potential application as an electron transport layer in single-junction perovskite solar cells (*31*). Koida *et al*. further elucidated the pivotal role of regulating oxygen stoichiometry in modulating the optoelectronic prop- erties of RPD-SnO*x* (*32*). Building upon these works, we de- veloped In-free tandem solar cells using RPD-SnO*x* with tailored optoelectronic properties as both the recombination layer and transparent electrodes. We present a comprehen- sive investigation of the optoelectronic properties, interfacial chemistry, charge recombination, and tandem performance enabled by RPD-SnO*x*. We achieved a champion PCE of 33.9% (certified 33.6%) for tandems with RPD-SnO*x* recombination layer (SnO*x*-RL tandem), and 33.2% for In-free tandems with RPD-SnO*x* recombination layer and electrodes (In-free tan- dem) measured with a mask area of 1 cm². An In-free mini- module fabricated from a single industrial-size tandem cell (21 × 10.5 cm²) attained a certified PCE of 31.0% and exhibited exceptional operational and damp-heat stability, retaining

95.2% of its initial PCE after 1,056 hours storage at 85% rela- tive humidity and 85°C.
## Performance of tandem solar cells

In monolithic tandem device with a SHJ bottom cell, three indium-based functional layers are typically used: front transparent electrode (F-TCO) under metal grid, recombina- tion layer (RL-TCO) that electrically connects the perovskite top cell with the SHJ bottom cell, and rear electrode (R-TCO) (Fig. 1A). Using double-side submicrometer-textured SHJ bot- tom cells, we fabricated SnO*x*-RL tandems and In-free tan- dems using RPD-SnO*x* recombination layer and electrodes, as well as control device using conventional IZO F-TCO (40 nm), ITO RL-TCO (20 nm) and R-TCO (80 nm). The detailed infor- mation for all TCOs is summarized in table S1. High-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron micros- copy (HAADF-STEM), combined with energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDX) elemental mapping, confirmed the thick- ness of the compact perovskite absorber to be about 800 nm as evidenced by the iodine signal, and resolved the thin func- tional layers on and under perovskite, including C₆₀, SnO*x*, Ag and MgF*x* (Fig. 1B). A conformal SnO*x* recombination layer (∼ 15 nm) uniformly coated the submicrometer surface textures, as evidenced by the homogeneous and continuous distribu- tion of Sn and O elements (fig. S1). The RPD-SnO*x* front elec- trode (50 nm) and the ALD-SnO*x* (15 nm) buffer layer formed an intimately integrated bilayer (∼ 65 nm) without a discern- ible interface, indicating excellent interlayer compatibility. Cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) further reveals high-quality perovskite compactly grown on the tex- tured SHJ bottom cell, characterized by vertically aligned, large grains spanning the full film thickness (fig. S2).

The illuminated current density-voltage (*J*-*V*) characteris- tics of champion devices revealed that the SnO*x*-RL tandem achieved a PCE of 33.9% (1 cm²), whereas the In-free tandem attained a slightly lower PCE of 33.2%. Both values exceeded that of the control device (32.7%) (Fig. 1C). Stabilized maxi- mum power point (MPP) tracking further confirmed PCEs of

33.8%, 33.1% and 32.6%, respectively, under standard AM1.5G spectrum for 300 s. The operational stability of the unencapsulated tandem solar cells was evaluated by maximum power point (MPP) tracking under continuous 1-sun illumination in nitrogen at- mosphere at room temperature (Fig. 1G). The control device degraded to ~ 50% of its initial PCE after 3,000 hours opera- tion. In contrast, the SnO*x*-RL tandem retained 70.8% of its initial PCE, whereas the In-free tandem exhibited enhanced stability, maintaining 81.1% of its initial PCE. The improved operational stability of the In-free tandem compared to the SnO*x*-RL tandem and the control device arose from the com- bined effects of robust interfacial chemistry and effective sup- pression of ion migration. SnO*x*-based devices benefitted from stronger SAM binding and reduced interfacial defect density enabled by the SnO*x*-RL, and the In-free tandem fur- ther eliminates indium-based TCOs that contain nanocrystal- line domains prone to halide diffusion (*35*). Fundamentally, in conventional TCOs, grain boundaries and defective amor- phous-crystalline interfaces act as fast channels for ion mi- gration. In contrast, the amorphous RPD-SnO*x* electrodes eliminates these pathways, thereby effectively block iodide migration from the perovskite film to the surface and miti- gate Ag electrode corrosion. Thus, the In-free tandem re- tained the highest fraction of its initial PCE under prolonged operation, followed by the SnO*x*-RL tandem, whereas the ITO/IZO-based control device degraded most rapidly.

## RPD-SnOx recombination layer

The recombination layer electrically connects the perov- skite top cell with the *c*-Si bottom cell (*4*) and enables effi- cient recombination of photogenerated holes and electrons across a SAM-modified interface (*36*). Our previous work has demonstrated RPD-SnO*x* as an effective transparent electrode for SHJ solar cells, with tunable optoelectronic properties and reduced deposition damage to underlying functional lay- ers (*24*, *34*). We adapted this approach to engineer SnO*x* re- combination layers for tandems with high carrier concentration by reducing the oxygen flow ratio during the RPD process. Hall effect measurements showed that the RPD- SnO*x* recombination layer shown higher carrier concentra- tion (1.18×10 21 cm −3 ) and lower carrier mobility (1.4 cm²/Vs) than sputtered ITO (table S2). SHJ bottom cells incorporating the RPD-SnO*x* recombination layer displayed a higher im- plied *V*oc than that with sputtered ITO (fig. S8), which partly accounted for the enhanced *V*oc observed in the tandems. We subsequently investigated the impact of SnO*x*-RL thickness on tandem performance. The results revealed that the devices with a 15 nm SnO*x*-RL achieve the maximum PCE, which is attributed to the superior *V*oc and FF (fig. S9). This represents a delicate balance between the optoelectronic properties of RPD-SnO*x* recombination layer and its synergistic impact on the overall performance of the tandem solar cells (figs. S10 and S11).

The interfacial charge transfer, device performance, and operational stability of tandem solar cells are strongly influ- enced by the coverage density and uniformity of SAMs on the recombination layer (*9*). We studied interfacial interaction by performing density functional theory (DFT) calculations that investigated the adsorption characteristics of the SAM mole- cule, [4-(3,6-dimethoxy-9H-carbazol-9-yl)ethyl] phosphonic acid (MeO-4PACz), on SnO*x* and ITO surfaces. The calculated charge density difference maps revealed more delocalized and spatially homogeneous charge redistribution for MeO- 4PACz adsorbed on SnO*x* than on ITO (Fig. 2A), where the cyan and yellow isosurfaces denote electron depletion and ac- cumulation, respectively. ## Industrial-size In-free tandem solar cells

We further validate the In-free tandem solar cells at an industrial scale using slot-die-coated perovskite films and an otherwise similar device architecture (21× 10.5 cm²) (fig. S24). The RPD-SnO*x* electrodes deposited under a higher oxygen flow ratio shown reduced carrier concentration but higher carrier mobility (table S2). The champion In-free tandem mini-module (active area of 207.9 cm²), fabricated from a sin- gle industrial-size device, achieved a certified PCE of 31.0% (Fig. 4A and fig. S25). The device yielded a stabilized power output of 6.39 W under MPP tracking, confirming a high PCE of 30.7% (fig. S26). Compared with laboratory-scale tandems, the PCE of the mini-module was primarily limited by a dra- matically reduced *J*sc of 18.9 mA/cm². The current densities integrated from the EQE spectra are 20.3 and 19.1 mA/cm² for the perovskite top cell and the SHJ bottom cell, respec- tively (Fig. 4B). Due to the localized beam spot size of the EQE measurement, the shading and reflection losses associated with the screen-printed fingers and ribbons are not captured in the integrated current density. This discrepancy indicated that in addition to parasitic absorption in the SnO*x* transpar- ent electrodes and encapsulation layers, reflection and shad- ing losses associated with the encapsulated glass and screen-

printed metal fingers constituted major contributors to the reduced current density in both sub-cells. To gain a deeper understanding of the cell-to-module current losses, we per- formed optical simulations on both the In-free small-area cell and the mini-module by combining ray tracing and transfer matrix methods (fig. S27). The results reveal that during the upscaling process from cell to mini-module, the primary rea- sons for the current reduction are the increased reflection loss caused by the module encapsulation (glass and TPO), and the front optical shading caused by the interconnection rib- bons and silver grids. Conversely, the parasitic absorption contributed by the SnO*x* recombination layer is substantially minimized and does not constitute a limiting factor for mod- Downloaded from [https://www.science.org](https://www.science.org) at University of Oxford on June 29, 2026 ules. The *V*oc of 1.94 V was lower than that of laboratory-scale tandems (1.97 V), likely associated with the larger spatial var- iation in defect density and interfacial quality that increased non-radiative recombination and reduced average QFLS. However, the FF increased to 84.3%, which partially compen- sated for the losses in *J*sc and *V*oc. This may arise from the pronounced current mismatch that shifts the operating point toward a higher FF regime (*50*), together with a combination of reduced shunt losses, edge recombination and optimized grid design enabled efficient lateral charge transport. To evaluate the long-term stability of the In-free tandem mini-modules, a series of complementary stability tests were conducted following the ISOS protocols. First, the storage sta- bility of mini-module was examined in N₂ box following the ISOS-D-1 protocol. The unencapsulated devices were stored in the dark under a controlled N₂ atmosphere and a relative humidity (RH) of below 0.001% at room temperature, with their PCEs measured at 12-day intervals. Both the In-free and SnO*x*-RL tandems exhibit excellent storage stability, retaining

99.0% and 91.4% of their initial PCEs after 3,168 hours of stor- age, respectively (Fig. 4C). The control device retains only
79.5% of its initial PCE over the same period. Subsequently, MPP tracking of the In-free and control mini-modules was performed at 85°C under continuous 1-sun illumination in N₂ atmosphere (ISOS-L-3, Fig. 4D). After 1,000 hours, the In-free device retained 93.9% of its initial PCE, demonstrating high operational stability under elevated-temperature conditions. In addition, the In-free tandem mini-module shown slight PCE degradation (-4.8%) after 1,056 hours test under damp- heat conditions following the ISOS-D-3 protocol (85% relative humidity and 85°C) (Fig. 4E), confirming its exceptional re- sistance to combined thermal and moisture stress. Finally, encapsulated In-free and control devices were de- ployed for outdoor field testing. After approximately 105 days of operation, including exposure to multiple rainy and over- cast days, the In-free mini-module retained 65% of its maxi- mum initial PCE, whereas the control device underwent rapid degradation to 5% of its maximum initial PCE after ap- proximately 75 days (Fig. 4F). The improved stability of the

In-free tandem mini-modules stemmed from the synergistic effects of robust interfacial chemistry, effective ion-migration blocking and intrinsically stable SnO*x* electrodes, mitigating non-radiative recombination, interfacial degradation and halide ion migration toward the electrodes during long-term operation. In addition, the reduced current density and im- proved thermal dissipation in the mini-module further alle- viate electrical and thermal stress, Thus, performance trade- offs associated with upscaling are fundamentally manageable and do not preclude high-efficiency tandem operation.

## Conclusions

We demonstrated a viable and scalable route toward high- efficiency and stable In-free tandem solar cells by replacing indium-based TCOs with RPD-SnO*x*. Beyond achieving state- of-the-art PCEs at both small and large scales, this work re- vealed that RPD-SnO*x* enables superior interfacial quality through dense SAM anchoring, suppressed non-radiative re- combination, and mitigated halide migration. The tandems not only exhibited high PCEs but also exceptional opera- tional, thermal and outdoor stability. By decoupling high per- formance from reliance on scarce and costly indium, this study establishes RPD-SnO*x* as a broadly applicable electrode and recombination layer for scalable, high-efficiency and du- rable tandem photovoltaics.
