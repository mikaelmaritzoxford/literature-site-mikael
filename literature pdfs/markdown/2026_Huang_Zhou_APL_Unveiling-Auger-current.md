### ABSTRACT

The rectification characteristics of semiconductor junctions are the basis for their application in diverse electronic and optoelectronic devices. Theoretically, the manifestation of Auger recombination under high positive bias voltage enables solar cells to achieve a high fill factor (FF); however, other recombination mechanisms and high series resistance make it difficult to be observed in the rectification curve. Here, the Auger recombination current component is identified in the rectification curves of silicon heterojunction solar cells featuring high FF and high open-circuit voltage (Voc). The current–voltage (I–V) characteristics of the device with high-conductivity nanocrystalline silicon oxide emitter demonstrate a distinct diode component exhibiting an ideality factor of n ¼ 2/3, which is a definitive indicator of Auger recombination. The temperature-dependent I–V characteristics and Suns-Voc measurements reveal that suppression of series resis tance and defect-mediated recombination is essential for unveiling the Auger recombination component.

Benefiting from the excellent passivation capability of intrinsic a-Si:H layers 1 and superior bulk quality of crystalline silicon substrates, both the investigated SHJ solar cells exhibit remarkably high minority carrier lifetimes (*>*3 ms). In this case, the SRH recombination related to bulk and surface defects is sufficiently suppressed in the SHJ solar cells despite the emitter difference. Moreover, as mentioned above, The Suns-Voc measurement eliminates the influence of series resistance. Therefore, the effects of Auger recombination become identified in the *iVoc* ln*(*u*)* curve under high injection conditions for both SHJ devices. Based on the dark I–V and Suns-Voc measurements, the Auger recombination current component is observed in the rectification curves of high-efficiency SHJ solar cells with the high-conductivity nc-SiOx:H(p þ ) emitter and high-quality intrinsic a-Si:H passivation layer. The factors that affect Auger recombination, such as series resis tance and defect-related recombination, have been clearly understood. The ability to distinguish and quantify Auger recombination in dark I–V measurements offers a powerful tool for advanced device diag nostics and material engineering in photovoltaics.
